Written 2026-11-11. Planning and synthesis: Claude Fable 5.2. Research workers: five Claude Opus 5.5 agents, one per topic. Question: for a Hokkaido ski trip, is December 2026 better or worse than January or February 2027?

For a picture version in plain language, see the explainer. Sections 9 and 10 were added later on 2026-11-11 from daily station data.

Answer

  1. Go from about 16 January to 3 February 2027. This period has the best odds of deep cold powder, a finished base and open gates. It is also between the New Year peak and the Lunar New Year peak. The best fortnight inside it is 19 January to 1 February (section 9).
  2. December is the weak month this winter. A record El Niño is in progress. In the Kutchan (Niseko) record, strong El Niño winters lose snow mainly in December: 4 of the 5 strongest had about two thirds of the usual December snowfall. December is also the month that starts with no base.
  3. The cold snap of 7-10 November does not change this. It was real (56 cm on the ground at Kutchan on 10 November), but early snow at town level melts. It was 32 cm one day later. JMA expects temperatures to go back to normal from mid November.
  4. February is the second choice. The base is at its deepest. Less new snow falls than in January, and recent El Niño Februaries were poor. Do not go on 4-11 February (Snow Festival, Lunar New Year and a national holiday in one week).
  5. The whole season leans a little below normal, not bad. JMA gives 40% below, 40% near and 20% above normal snowfall for the Hokkaido Sea of Japan side. A normal Niseko winter is still about 9 m of snowfall in the town.

Confidence: high that January is the safest month (this is climatology and is true every year). Medium that December will be below normal. Low on the season total, because this El Niño is stronger than any past event.

2. What El Niño does to Hokkaido snow

The official statistics show no reliable signal for Hokkaido. JMA's table of significant El Niño winter effects (1947/48 to 2020/21) lists a warm West Japan and a wet Pacific side of East Japan. It lists nothing for North Japan temperature or for Sea of Japan side snowfall. V JMA, El Niño and Japan's weather

The mechanism points to a weaker winter monsoon. El Niño builds a high near the Philippines and weakens the north-west monsoon wind that makes sea-effect snow.

  • Wang, Wu and Fu (2000), the Pacific-East Asian teleconnection. S abstract
  • Ohba and Sugimoto (2021): El Niño gives less precipitation on the Sea of Japan side and more on the Pacific side. The link is stronger when the PDO is negative, as it is now. S Climate Dynamics
  • Ueda et al. (2015): Sea of Japan side snowfall follows tropical convection. S Int. J. Climatol.
  • Yatagai and Kinoshita (2022), Sapporo area: ENSO changes where the snow falls more than how much. El Niño favours the south-west mountains and inland. S Atmosphere 13:1413

Our own count from the JMA Kutchan record. Kutchan is the JMA station in the valley below Niseko. kutchan-snowfall.csv holds its monthly snowfall totals for 65 winters (1961-62 to 2025-26). V JMA Kutchan monthly snowfall enso_composite.py compares each winter with the median of the ten winters around it. This removes the long downward drift.

What this shows:

  • Across all El Niño winters the effect is close to zero. A ski site found the same from 73 seasons (El Niño 11.45 m, La Niña 11.57 m). S skiasia
  • Strong events are different. 7 of 8 were below their neighbours for the three months, by 8% at the median.
  • The loss is in December. Of the 5 strongest events (ONI peak of 1.8 or more), 4 had 61% to 66% of the usual December snowfall (1972, 1997, 2015, 2023). January and February were near normal at the median. January 1998 was 132%.
  • 1982-83 was a snowy exception, so a good December is still possible.

Limits: 8 winters is a small sample. The differences are inside the normal year-to-year spread (about ±25% for one month). The ENSO year lists in the script are the usual NOAA classification, entered from memory M. Raw values in the yearly table do not match the JMA normals (the 1991-2020 average of raw December values is 298 cm, the normal is 253 cm). JMA probably adjusted the normals for a change in measuring method. Compare the yearly table only with itself.

JMA's reasons: the record El Niño continues. The Siberian High is near normal, but the Aleutian Low is weak on its west side, so the monsoon wind near Japan is weak. The whole atmosphere is warm. JMA's summary for Hokkaido: temperature normal or above, Sea of Japan side snowfall normal or below.

More precipitation with less snowfall means more of it falls as rain or wet snow at low level. This matters most at the start and end of the season and at the base of the resorts.

4. The November cold snap

  • JMA issued an Early Warning Information on 2 November for Hokkaido and Tohoku. It warned of markedly low temperature from about 8 November. S news report. The bulletin itself is no longer on the JMA feed.
  • "Once in ten years" has a narrow meaning. JMA issues this bulletin when there is a 30% or higher chance that a 5-day mean falls in the coldest 10% for that time of year. V criteria on the JMA early warning page. It is a cold week for early November, not a rare winter event.
  • The 1-month outlook of 5 November gave an 80% chance of below-normal temperature in week 1, then near normal. V JMA 1-month forecast
  • What happened at Kutchan: no snow on 1-6 November, then 13 cm, 7 cm, 38 cm and 8 cm of new snow on 7-10 November. Depth was 56 cm on the 10th and 32 cm at 19:00 on the 11th. V JMA Kutchan daily data. The normal deepest snow for all of November is 36 cm.
  • Sapporo had 10 cm on 7 November S tenki.jp, 2026-11-07 and 0 cm on 11 November V JMA AMeDAS map.

Reading: it helps the early base high on the mountain and it supports an on-time opening. It says very little about December to February. Last winter shows this: Kutchan had 56 cm on 12 December 2025 and 21 cm on 25 December. S skiasia

5. Other drivers

Driver State Effect on Hokkaido Source
Sea of Japan surface temperature Much above normal in October, with a much stronger Tsushima Warm Current. The cold snap shrank the very warm area. Next month: north normal or above, south much above. Each cold outbreak picks up more moisture, so single storms can be heavy. Early in the season the same warm water gives wetter snow and rain at low level. V JMA sea diagnosis, 2026-11-10, Sea of Japan page
Arctic Oscillation +1.29 for October. It fell to about -2 on 10 November. The forecast mean goes back to about 0 by 15-17 November, with a wide spread. Negative AO goes with a stronger Siberian High and colder East Asia (S Gong, Wang and Zhu 2001; M DOI). It explains the cold snap. It is not predictable beyond about two weeks. V NOAA CPC AO
Stratospheric polar vortex The wind at 60°N, 10 hPa fell from 21.9 m/s on 23 October to 0.3 m/s on 5 November. The normal for that date is 26.0 and the lowest value in the record before this year (1978 onward) was 13.6. The forecast gives a reversal (-3.5 m/s on 11 November) and a recovery to 14 m/s by 19 November. A reversal is a major sudden stratospheric warming. After such an event, cold outbreaks at the surface are more likely for several weeks. This is the main reason December could beat the El Niño odds. Where the cold goes is not known in advance. V NASA Ozone Watch, MERRA-2 and GEOS FP (file u60n_10_2026_merra2.txt); news context S phys.org
Barents-Kara sea ice Very low in October. Some studies link low ice to cold Eurasian winters (S Mori et al. 2014, 2019, news summary; M DOIs 2014, 2019). Others disagree (S Blackport et al. 2019, cited in this review; M DOI). Weak evidence. S Copernicus, October 2026
Long-term warming Total snowfall goes down. The heaviest single falls inland go up. Fewer snow days, some very large ones. Low-level snow is less reliable in December and March. S Kawase et al., via PEPS 2023; record fall at Obihiro in February 2025 S Hirata et al., SOLA 2025

Net effect: the non-ENSO drivers favour sharp cold outbreaks with heavy falls, mainly in the next four to six weeks. The El Niño background favours long mild breaks between them.

6b. West against central Hokkaido: Niseko and Furano

Claim to test: central Hokkaido is less deep but more consistent.

  • Less deep: true. Furano has about half the snowfall and less than half the base in every month.
  • More consistent: partly. New snow is a little steadier in December and January. The base is not steadier. It changes as much as at Kutchan in relative terms.
  • Strong El Niño winters were kinder to Furano. Snowfall as % of the winters around it, for 1982, 1991, 1997, 2009, 2015 and 2023: December 148, 81, 92, 107, 91, 76 at Furano against 153, 109, 63, 87, 64, 66 at Kutchan. January 155, 143, 108, 118, 123, 128 at Furano (above usual in all six) against 85, 68, 132, 110, 101, 97 at Kutchan. This agrees with Yatagai and Kinoshita (2022): El Niño moves snow toward the inland side. Six winters is a small sample.
  • December snowfall at the two stations has a correlation of 0.11. A poor December at Niseko says little about Furano.

Result: Furano is a real hedge for this winter. It gives colder, drier snow and less El Niño risk, with a much thinner base and less off-piste depth. Furano's snowfall record falls after about 2008. The station history was not checked, so do not read that step as climate.

Ranking for this winter:

  1. 16 January to 3 February. Best snow odds and no peak week. After 26 January it is also quieter.
  2. 12 to 28 February. Deep base, fewer people. Higher risk of a mild spell. Central Hokkaido (Furano, Tomamu, Asahidake) is colder and keeps its snow better then.
  3. Late December. Possible, but it is the most expensive week and it has the weakest odds this winter.
  4. Early to mid December. Only as a cheap trip booked late with free cancellation, after you see the base.

If the dates must be in December: wait for the JMA 3-month outlook on 24 November and the Kutchan depth in the first week of December. A depth near 60 cm or more at Kutchan by 10 December, with cold air in the 1-month outlook, is a good sign. Choose Kiroro or Asahidake, which build a base earlier than the Niseko base areas. S Powderhounds, Hokkaido

"Low" and "high" are the 10th and 90th percentiles of daily depth. "Strong El Niño" is the median of 1991, 1997, 2009, 2015 and 2023.

  • Powder. The chance of a 20 cm day is 13% to 19% from 8 December to the end of January. It is about 10% in February and 4% in the last week.
  • Thaws. One day in three is a thaw day in the first week of December. From 5 January to 8 February it is one day in twenty or fewer.
  • Base. The typical base passes 1 m in the week of 5 January. In a poor year (the low column) it does so in the week of 26 January.
  • Sunshine. January is dark: about one day in four has 3 hours of sun. The last week of February has one in two. Furano has more sun in every week (about 40% of days from mid January).
  • Strong El Niño winters. The base was about two weeks late and stayed 20 to 30 cm lower all season. December thaw days were about double (43%, 26% and 29% in the first three weeks). The weeks of 19 and 26 January were the snowiest, with 90 and 80 cm of new snow against a usual 64 and 67 cm. Five winters is a small sample.
  • Wind. The share of days with a maximum wind of 8 m/s or more is 27% to 41% in every week, with no pattern. Town wind does not show lift closures on the mountain. This question stays open.

Result: 19 January to 1 February is the best fortnight. It has a full base in 9 winters out of 10, almost no thaws, steady powder odds and the most new snow in strong El Niño winters.

Measuring change at Kutchan. JMA marks the Kutchan snowfall series as not homogeneous from the cold season of 2005-06 (an instrument or method change). The deepest-snow series has no such mark. V JMA Kutchan annual page weekly_odds.py never compares a winter with neighbours on the other side of that change. With this rule the Kutchan December figure for strong El Niño winters is 80% (section 2 gives 77% from the longer record without the rule). The per-winter values for 1997, 2015 and 2023 stay at 63% to 66%. The long downward drift in the raw Kutchan snowfall series is partly this change, so do not read it as climate.

What could not be verified

  • Snow on the mountain. Every station record in this report is from a valley town (Kutchan is at about 175 m M). No official long daily record exists on the Niseko slopes. The slopes hold more snow than the town and keep it longer, and a warm December melts more in the valley than up high. The town record compares winters with each other. It does not give the depth on the slopes.
