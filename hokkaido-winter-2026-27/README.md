# Hokkaido ski winter 2026-27: forecast and month choice

Written 2026-11-11. Planning and synthesis: Claude Fable 5.2. Research
workers: five Claude Opus 5.5 agents, one per topic. Question: for a
Hokkaido ski trip, is December 2026 better or worse than January or
February 2027?

For a picture version in plain language, open `explainer.html` in a
browser. Sections 9 and 10 were added later on 2026-11-11 from daily
station data.

## Answer

1. **Go from about 16 January to 3 February 2027.** This period has the
   best odds of deep cold powder, a finished base and open gates. It is
   also between the New Year peak and the Lunar New Year peak. The best
   fortnight inside it is 19 January to 1 February (section 9).
2. **December is the weak month this winter.** A record El Niño is in
   progress. In the Kutchan (Niseko) record, strong El Niño winters lose
   snow mainly in December: 4 of the 5 strongest had about two thirds of
   the usual December snowfall. December is also the month that starts with
   no base.
3. **The cold snap of 7-10 November does not change this.** It was real
   (56 cm on the ground at Kutchan on 10 November), but early snow at
   town level melts. It was 32 cm one day later. JMA expects temperatures
   to go back to normal from mid November.
4. **February is the second choice.** The base is at its deepest. Less new
   snow falls than in January, and recent El Niño Februaries were poor.
   Do not go on 4-11 February (Snow Festival, Lunar New Year and a
   national holiday in one week).
5. **The whole season leans a little below normal, not bad.** JMA gives
   40% below, 40% near and 20% above normal snowfall for the Hokkaido Sea
   of Japan side. A normal Niseko winter is still about 9 m of snowfall
   in the town.

Confidence: high that January is the safest month (this is climatology and
is true every year). Medium that December will be below normal. Low on
the season total, because this El Niño is stronger than any past event.

## 2. What El Niño does to Hokkaido snow

**The official statistics show no reliable signal for Hokkaido.** JMA's
table of significant El Niño winter effects (1947/48 to 2020/21) lists a
warm West Japan and a wet Pacific side of East Japan. It lists nothing
for North Japan temperature or for Sea of Japan side snowfall.
[V] [JMA, El Niño and Japan's weather](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/cpd/data/elnino/learning/tenkou/nihon1.html)

**The mechanism points to a weaker winter monsoon.** El Niño builds a
high near the Philippines and weakens the north-west monsoon wind that
makes sea-effect snow.

- Wang, Wu and Fu (2000), the Pacific-East Asian teleconnection. [S] [abstract](https://ams.confex.com/ams/annual2000/techprogram/paper_5534.htm)
- Ohba and Sugimoto (2021): El Niño gives less precipitation on the Sea of
  Japan side and more on the Pacific side. The link is stronger when the
  PDO is negative, as it is now. [S] [Climate Dynamics](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00382-021-06052-9)
- Ueda et al. (2015): Sea of Japan side snowfall follows tropical
  convection. [S] [Int. J. Climatol.](https://rmets.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/joc.4032)
- Yatagai and Kinoshita (2022), Sapporo area: ENSO changes where the snow
  falls more than how much. El Niño favours the south-west mountains and
  inland. [S] [Atmosphere 13:1413](https://doi.org/10.3390/atmos13091413)

**Our own count from the JMA Kutchan record.** Kutchan is the JMA station
in the valley below Niseko. `kutchan-snowfall.csv` holds its monthly
snowfall totals for 65 winters (1961-62 to 2025-26).
[V] [JMA Kutchan monthly snowfall](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/monthly_s3.php?prec_no=16&block_no=47433&view=p6)
`enso_composite.py` compares each winter with the median of the ten
winters around it. This removes the long downward drift.

| 2015-16 | 64% | 86% |
| 2023-24 | 66% | 80% |

What this shows:

- Across all El Niño winters the effect is close to zero. A ski site
  found the same from 73 seasons (El Niño 11.45 m, La Niña 11.57 m).
  [S] [skiasia](https://skiasia.com/news/worried-about-the-effect-of-the-super-el-nino-on-your-niseko-ski-holiday-you-shouldnt-be/)
- Strong events are different. 7 of 8 were below their neighbours for the
  three months, by 8% at the median.
- The loss is in December. Of the 5 strongest events (ONI peak of 1.8 or
  more), 4 had 61% to 66% of the usual December snowfall (1972, 1997,
  2015, 2023). January and February were near normal at
  the median. January 1998 was 132%.
- 1982-83 was a snowy exception, so a good December is still possible.

Limits: 8 winters is a small sample. The differences are inside the
normal year-to-year spread (about ±25% for one month). The ENSO year
lists in the script are the usual NOAA classification, entered from
memory [M]. Raw values in the yearly table do not match the JMA normals
(the 1991-2020 average of raw December values is 298 cm, the normal is
253 cm). JMA probably adjusted the normals for a change in measuring
method. Compare the yearly table only with itself.

## 4. The November cold snap

- JMA issued an Early Warning Information on 2 November for Hokkaido and
  Tohoku. It warned of markedly low temperature from about 8 November.
  [S] [news report](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/articles/37bb2f340bf4e1cdca706b91fe8d164adfbeb7b0).
  The bulletin itself is no longer on the JMA feed.
- "Once in ten years" has a narrow meaning. JMA issues this bulletin when
  there is a 30% or higher chance that a 5-day mean falls in the coldest
  10% **for that time of year**. [V] criteria on the
  [JMA early warning page](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/season/). It is a
  cold week for early November, not a rare winter event.
- The 1-month outlook of 5 November gave an 80% chance of below-normal
  temperature in week 1, then near normal.
  [V] [JMA 1-month forecast](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/season/)
- What happened at Kutchan: no snow on 1-6 November, then 13 cm, 7 cm,
  38 cm and 8 cm of new snow on 7-10 November. Depth was 56 cm on the
  10th and 32 cm at 19:00 on the 11th.
  [V] [JMA Kutchan daily data](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/daily_s1.php?prec_no=16&block_no=47433&year=2026&month=11).
  The normal deepest snow for all of November is 36 cm.
- Sapporo had 10 cm on 7 November
  [S] [tenki.jp, 2026-11-07](https://tenki.jp/forecaster/deskpart/2026/11/07/41234.html)
  and 0 cm on 11 November [V] [JMA AMeDAS map](https://www.jma.go.jp/bosai/amedas/).

Reading: it helps the early base high on the mountain and it supports an
on-time opening. It says very little about December to February. Last
winter shows this: Kutchan had 56 cm on 12 December 2025 and 21 cm on
25 December.
[S] [skiasia](https://skiasia.com/news/has-niseko-just-recorded-its-worst-start-to-winter-in-67-years/)

## 6b. West against central Hokkaido: Niseko and Furano

Claim to test: central Hokkaido is less deep but more consistent.

Data: JMA Furano (AMeDAS, 174 m) and JMA Kutchan, winters 1991-92 to
2025-26. `furano-snowfall.csv` and `deepest-snow.csv` hold the tables.
[V] [Furano normals](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/nml_amd_ym.php?prec_no=12&block_no=0021),
[V] [Furano yearly pages](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/monthly_a1.php?prec_no=12&block_no=0021&year=2025) (one page per year; change `year=`),
[V] [Kutchan yearly pages](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/monthly_s1.php?prec_no=16&block_no=47433&year=2025) (same).
Both stations are in valley towns. The mountains hold more snow.

| | Kutchan | Furano |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January mean temperature (normal) | -5.4 °C | -8.3 °C |
| Dec-Feb snowfall, average | 809 cm | 411 cm |
| Deepest snow, Dec / Jan / Feb average | 100 / 157 / 184 cm | 45 / 66 / 74 cm |
| Deepest snow, lowest year, Dec / Jan / Feb | 51 / 83 / 95 cm | 14 / 44 / 52 cm |
| Year-to-year spread of deepest snow (standard deviation / mean), Dec / Jan / Feb | 0.29 / 0.20 / 0.17 | 0.31 / 0.21 / 0.18 |
| Year-to-year spread of monthly snowfall, trend removed, Dec / Jan / Feb | 0.23 / 0.23 / 0.23 | 0.18 / 0.18 / 0.22 |

- **Less deep: true.** Furano has about half the snowfall and less than
  half the base in every month.
- **More consistent: partly.** New snow is a little steadier in December
  and January. The base is not steadier. It changes as much as at
  Kutchan in relative terms.
- **Strong El Niño winters were kinder to Furano.** Snowfall as % of the
  winters around it, for 1982, 1991, 1997, 2009, 2015 and 2023:
  December 148, 81, 92, 107, 91, 76 at Furano against 153, 109, 63, 87,
  64, 66 at Kutchan. January 155, 143, 108, 118, 123, 128 at Furano
  (above usual in all six) against 85, 68, 132, 110, 101, 97 at Kutchan.
  This agrees with Yatagai and Kinoshita (2022): El Niño moves snow
  toward the inland side. Six winters is a small sample.
- December snowfall at the two stations has a correlation of 0.11. A
  poor December at Niseko says little about Furano.

Result: Furano is a real hedge for this winter. It gives colder, drier
snow and less El Niño risk, with a much thinner base and less off-piste
depth. Furano's snowfall record falls after about 2008. The station
history was not checked, so do not read that step as climate.

## 8. Forecast by month

These are my estimates. They combine the JMA outlook, the Kutchan
composites and the base build-up. They are not an official forecast.

| | December 2026 | January 2027 | February 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Snowfall against normal | Below normal is most likely. About 50 / 30 / 20 (below / near / above) | Near normal. About 35 / 40 / 25 | Near to a little below. About 40 / 40 / 20 |
| Base | Thin until the last third of the month | Complete by mid month in most years | Deepest |
| Snow quality | Mixed. Rain or wet snow is possible at the base | Best | Good, with more mild breaks late in the month |
| Main risk | Closed gates and thin cover on a non-refundable booking | Storm days and wind holds | A mild spell or a long dry gap |
| What could make it better | The polar vortex weakening and a warm Sea of Japan give large early storms, as in 1982 | - | A late cold outbreak |
| Crowds and price | Cheap to 19 Dec, then the peak | Peak to 5 Jan, then moderate, best after 26 Jan | Very busy 4-11 Feb, then moderate |

Ranking for this winter:

1. **16 January to 3 February.** Best snow odds and no peak week. After
   26 January it is also quieter.
2. **12 to 28 February.** Deep base, fewer people. Higher risk of a mild
   spell. Central Hokkaido (Furano, Tomamu, Asahidake) is colder and
   keeps its snow better then.
3. **Late December.** Possible, but it is the most expensive week and it
   has the weakest odds this winter.
4. **Early to mid December.** Only as a cheap trip booked late with free
   cancellation, after you see the base.

If the dates must be in December: wait for the JMA 3-month outlook on
24 November and the Kutchan depth in the first week of December. A depth
near 60 cm or more at Kutchan by 10 December, with cold air in the
1-month outlook, is a good sign. Choose Kiroro or Asahidake, which build
a base earlier than the Niseko base areas.
[S] [Powderhounds, Hokkaido](https://www.powderhounds.com/japan/hokkaido.aspx)

## 9. Week by week at Kutchan

`weekly_odds.py` reads the daily records in `data/` (JMA Kutchan, 1961 to
2026; JMA Furano, 1981 to 2026) and writes `analysis.json`.
[V] [JMA Kutchan daily pages](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/daily_s1.php?prec_no=16&block_no=47433&year=2026&month=11),
[V] [JMA Furano daily pages](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/stats/etrn/view/daily_a1.php?prec_no=12&block_no=0021&year=2026&month=1)
(one page per month; change `year=` and `month=`). Weeks are
7-day blocks from 1 December. The table uses winters 1991-92 to 2025-26.

- **Powder.** The chance of a 20 cm day is 13% to 19% from 8 December to
  the end of January. It is about 10% in February and 4% in the last
  week.
- **Thaws.** One day in three is a thaw day in the first week of
  December. From 5 January to 8 February it is one day in twenty or
  fewer.
- **Base.** The typical base passes 1 m in the week of 5 January. In a
  poor year (the low column) it does so in the week of 26 January.
- **Sunshine.** January is dark: about one day in four has 3 hours of
  sun. The last week of February has one in two. Furano has more sun in
  every week (about 40% of days from mid January).
- **Strong El Niño winters.** The base was about two weeks late and
  stayed 20 to 30 cm lower all season. December thaw days were about
  double (43%, 26% and 29% in the first three weeks). The weeks of
  19 and 26 January were the snowiest, with 90 and 80 cm of new snow
  against a usual 64 and 67 cm. Five winters is a small sample.
- **Wind.** The share of days with a maximum wind of 8 m/s or more is
  27% to 41% in every week, with no pattern. Town wind does not show
  lift closures on the mountain. This question stays open.

Result: 19 January to 1 February is the best fortnight. It has a full
base in 9 winters out of 10, almost no thaws, steady powder odds and
the most new snow in strong El Niño winters.

change). The deepest-snow series has no such mark.

series is partly this change, so do not read it as climate.

## What could not be verified

- Snow on the mountain. Every station record in this report is from a
  valley town (Kutchan is at about 175 m [M]). No official long daily
  record exists on the Niseko slopes. The slopes hold more snow than the
  town and keep it longer, and a warm December melts more in the valley
  than up high. The town record compares winters with each other. It does
  not give the depth on the slopes.

- The text of the 2 November early warning (news reports only).
- China's 2027 Spring Festival holiday schedule and the Rusutsu opening
  date (not published yet).
- IRI, BoM, QBO and PDO values (search text only).
- Sea of Japan anomaly in degrees (JMA gives it only on maps).
- Lift closures by wind on the mountain (no public record found).
- The research papers in section 2 (abstracts seen as search text).
