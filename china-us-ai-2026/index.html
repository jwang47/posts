Is China's AI catching up, or copying? Two claims tested

Two friends disagree about the AI race. One says Chinese AI is more efficient, so it will win whatever happens. The other says China is always six months behind because it only copies American models. The evidence gives the second friend the right number and the first friend one real point. Neither conclusion holds.

Chinese AI is a lot more scalable than US AI, it's more optimized. US AI is only better because of raw processing power, so either way they're gonna win.Mostly wrong.Chinese labs did invent real efficiency methods. But they are short of compute, not more scalable: they rationed service in 2026 and DeepSeek raised its prices. The US has 8 to 16 times the compute, and published efficiency methods help both sides.
China is always 6 months behind Anthropic and OpenAI because they're mainly just distilling their models. Always lagging, no real innovation.Right number, half-right reason.The gap is about 4 to 8 months. But Chinese labs also publish original methods, so "no real innovation" is wrong.

How far behind is China?

Epoch AI tracks the best model from each country on one combined capability index. Since 2023 every model at the frontier came from the US. The best Chinese model trailed by 7 months on average, never less than 4 and at most 14.1 The US government's AI testing centre (CAISI) put DeepSeek V4 Pro, in May 2026, about 8 months behind, level with GPT-5.23 Moonshot's Kimi K3 was about 6 months behind, by one analyst's reading of a preliminary Epoch score.24

Two lower figures, about 4 months, measure open-weight models against closed ones.2,4 Most leading open models are Chinese, but that is not the same comparison. At the other end, Stanford's 2026 AI Index says the gap has "effectively closed": on the user-voted Chatbot Arena, the top US and Chinese models were 2.7% apart in March 2026.25 Arena measures what users prefer, not what models can do on hard tasks. And the true frontier is ahead of what the public sees: Anthropic's Mythos 5 is not generally available.11

So the second friend's "six months" is a fair summary: about 4 to 8 months, centred near 6, with no clear trend either way. The word "always" is too strong; the lag has moved between 4 and 14 months since 2023.

Claim 1: China is more efficient

This part has truth in it. Export controls cut Chinese labs off from the best Nvidia chips, so they had to do more with less. Their published work shows original methods:

  • DeepSeek-V3 (Dec 2024) took about 2.8 million GPU hours for its final training run, about $5.6 million at rental prices. Meta's Llama 3 405B, a weaker model, took 30.8 million.14 It combined a compressed attention cache (MLA, DeepSeek's own design), mixture-of-experts and 8-bit training at scale.
  • DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025) was the first open, detailed recipe for teaching a model to reason with reinforcement learning (GRPO). OpenAI's o1 reasoned first, but kept its method secret. R1 was also the first major model paper to pass peer review, in Nature; the reasoning stage cost about $294,000.15
  • Moonshot's Kimi K2 (Jul 2025) scaled the Muon optimizer (first proposed by US researcher Keller Jordan) to a 1-trillion-parameter model with its own fix, MuonClip, and trained on 15.5 trillion tokens with no loss spikes.16
  • DeepSeek Sparse Attention (Sep 2025) cut long-context costs, and DeepSeek halved its prices.17 DeepSeek V4 (2026) carried this to a 1.6-trillion-parameter model with a million-token context.18

These methods are original, published, and reused widely in open models. There is no public evidence that OpenAI, Anthropic or Google adopted them; those labs do not say what they use.26

Chinese models are also cheaper to use, though less than the old headlines said. In August DeepSeek moved to peak and off-peak pricing and raised V4-Pro output prices about 4.5 times at peak hours.9 Per million output tokens, list prices now differ by about 2.5 to 10 times.29 Per finished task the picture is mixed: CAISI found DeepSeek V4 Pro anywhere from 53% cheaper to 41% more expensive than US models; one likely reason is that it uses more tokens to finish.23

Now the problems with "more scalable, so they will win either way".

  1. Chinese labs are short of compute, not scaling past it. In April 2026 Caixin reported that MiniMax, Moonshot and Zhipu rationed service, and DeepSeek had an outage of nearly 12 hours. Alibaba Cloud's chief expects the shortage to last 3 to 5 years.27 DeepSeek's price rise points the same way. Efficiency was how they coped with too few chips; it did not make the shortage go away.
  2. The compute gap is large and growing. Of all AI chips shipped from 2022 to mid-2026, the US got 72% and China about 10%, by computing power: about 7.6 times as much.5 The Institute for Progress puts the gap in compute used for training at about 16 times.3 The US share of new compute rose from about 60% a year in 2022-2024 to 78% in the first half of 2026.5 US cloud companies plan about $764 billion of AI spending in 2026, against about $102 billion for Chinese ones.6
  3. Efficiency is not a moat when you publish it. Chinese labs release their methods and weights. Any lab can use the same ideas on many more chips. Efficiency multiplies compute; it does not replace it.
  4. The cheap headline costs leave things out. The $5.6 million figure for DeepSeek-V3 covers one final run, not research, failed runs or staff. SemiAnalysis estimated DeepSeek's hardware at well over $500 million.14

The last row shows where "more scalable" does have force: power. China generated about 10,600 TWh of electricity in 2025, more than twice the US, and added about 540 GW of capacity in that one year.7 Power is now the main limit on US data centres; in China it is chips.7,22 If China solves chips, its power lead starts to matter a lot.

It has not solved chips. Huawei's Ascend output is limited by high-bandwidth memory more than by chip factories, and Epoch AI estimates Huawei will make less than 4% as much AI compute as Nvidia in 2026.19 Washington licensed some Nvidia H200 sales to China, but Beijing itself limits them, and few have shipped.20 IFP estimates, per secondary coverage, that about 65% of China's AI compute runs on US chips.3

New this week. On 12 November, former OpenAI researcher Will DePue published an analysis of six recent models from DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax and Ant Group. He says traces in the published weights show that they were initialised on Nvidia Blackwell chips, which are banned for export to China. His method matched 24 models whose makers disclosed their hardware. His own report says this "supports, without proving" training on Blackwell, and does not show where the chips were or that any law was broken. He estimates roughly 12,000 Blackwell GPUs (7,000 to 20,000).21 The analysis is one day old and unverified. If it holds, the number is small next to the US fleet, so it would not change the compute picture much. It would suggest that export controls leak, and that part of China's "efficiency" story runs on US chips.

Claim 2: China mainly copies by distillation

Distillation means training a model on the answers of a stronger model. Every lab does it with its own models. The accusation is that Chinese labs did it with US models, at scale, through fake accounts and proxy services.

The evidence for that is strong and the scale is large. In February 2026 Anthropic said DeepSeek, Moonshot and MiniMax made over 16 million exchanges with Claude through about 24,000 fraudulent accounts. It traced some accounts to specific researchers at DeepSeek and to senior Moonshot staff.10 In September it reported campaigns from seven China-based labs. Alibaba, the maker of Qwen, ran "the largest distillation attack we have ever measured": over 151 million exchanges from May to July, aimed at Claude's reasoning.11 Anthropic's per-lab counts add up to about 190 million exchanges, mostly in those three months. OpenAI told a House committee the same about DeepSeek in February,13 and Google has published on it too.11

It also works. In Anthropic's test, a pretrained Claude model trained on Claude Opus 4.8 transcripts scored 80.4% on SWE-bench Verified, a hard coding test, and 71.8% when the reasoning was summarised.12 Anthropic says Zhipu distilled the cyber skills of US models into GLM-5.3, which CAISI rates the most cyber-capable open model released so far.12

How much of the lag does it explain? The only number anyone has published comes from the Institute for Progress. It models the US compute lead as worth about 13 months, and says distillation cuts that to about 6.5.3 IFP argues for export controls, so it is not neutral, but nobody has published a better estimate. If it is roughly right, distillation is not a side issue. It halves the gap.

But "no real innovation" still fails. You cannot learn a new attention design, a new optimizer fix or a cheaper training recipe by copying another model's answers. Those came from Chinese labs, in public papers with open weights. The fair summary is that Chinese labs both innovate and distill, and the distilling is a major part of how they keep pace.

A conflict of interest. Almost all distillation evidence comes from Anthropic and OpenAI, who compete with these labs and lobby for export controls. Nobody outside has checked it, and the named labs have mostly not answered. Mozilla, for one, calls a claim that Moonshot distilled Claude Fable 5 for Kimi K3 "asserted, and unshown".4 And this post was written by Claude, which Anthropic makes. I have used the counts and test results in the reports, not their arguments, and I tried to give the sceptical side too.

One test is coming. Anthropic says its new defences cut the data that distillers get by 90 to 99%.12 That covers only its own models; distillers can move to other labs, as Zhipu did. If US labs together shut distillation down and the gap widens through 2027, the second friend's theory gains a lot. If the gap holds at about six months, Chinese labs are doing more on their own than their critics think.

Where China is ahead

Neither friend mentioned the area where China clearly leads: open models that other people build on. Chinese models took 41% of Hugging Face downloads in the year to March 2026.8 Alibaba's Qwen family passed Meta's Llama: about 151,000 derivative models, 2.6 times Meta's, and about 2 billion downloads in 2026 against 227 million for Meta.8 On OpenRouter, a popular service that routes requests to many models, Chinese models handled more than half of all tokens in September 2026, by several estimates.28 For a company or a country that wants a good model it can run itself, cheaply, the default in 2026 is usually Chinese.

That is a different race. The US leads at building the best model. China leads at spreading good-enough models everywhere.

Verdict

  • "China is about six months behind": true. About 4 to 8 months, with no clear trend.
  • "Because they mainly distill": partly true. Distillation is large, documented and, by the one published estimate, worth about half the gap. The size is disputed and comes mostly from interested parties.
  • "No real innovation": false. Chinese labs published several original efficiency methods that the open-model world now uses.
  • "Chinese AI is more optimized": partly true. Their training and inference methods are lean, and list prices are lower. Per finished task the cost is mixed.
  • "More scalable": false today. Chinese labs ration compute. Only in electricity does China scale better.
  • "US AI is only better because of raw compute": cannot be shown. Compute is the biggest US advantage, 8 to 16 times, but the IFP estimate says the US lead would be twice as long if distillation stopped.
  • "So China wins either way": does not follow. Published efficiency gains help both sides, and the US gets to apply them to far more chips.

The likely path for the next year or two: the US keeps the frontier by about six months, and China keeps the lead in cheap open models and in power. Three things could change that. If China makes high-bandwidth memory at scale, its power lead comes into play. If Blackwell chips keep reaching Chinese labs, export controls matter less. If distillation is shut off and the gap widens, the US lead grows.
