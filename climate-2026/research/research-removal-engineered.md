## 1. How much removal is needed

### IPCC AR6 WG3

- CDR is needed to counterbalance residual emissions for net zero CO2 and net zero GHG; AR6 SPM C.11 says CDR is "unavoidable" to reach net zero. UNVERIFIED [M] (AR6 WG3 SPM, 2022, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/).
- 1.5 °C pathways: total CDR 5-16 GtCO2/yr in 2050 (secondary summary of the SPM). Cumulative 2020-2100: BECCS 30-780 GtCO2, DACCS 0-310 GtCO2. VERIFIED [S] — secondary source (Zero Carbon Analytics, https://zerocarbon-analytics.org/nature/ipcc-working-group-iii-report-bioenergy-with-carbon-capture-and-storage-beccs/). Check against SPM C.3 / Table SPM.
- Scenarios likely below 2 °C or lower, 2050 medians: BECCS 2.75 (0.52-9.45) GtCO2/yr; DACCS 0.02 (0-1.74) GtCO2/yr. Cumulative 2020-2100: BECCS 328 (168-763) GtCO2; DACCS 29 (0-339) GtCO2. DACCS potential 5-40 GtCO2/yr, limited by low-carbon energy and cost. VERIFIED [S] — but from a draft text of Chapter 12 (https://plaintextipcc.nfshost.com/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_Chapter_12.html). Check the final Chapter 12 (https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-12/).
- AR6 scenario database: 516 of 540 scenarios with novel CDR have BECCS, only 4 have DACCS; most AR6 scenarios use BECCS as the only novel method. VERIFIED [S] (Prütz et al. 2023, Environ. Res. Commun. 5 041005, https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/18767/1/Pr%C3%BCtz_2023_Environ._Res._Commun._5_041005.pdf; and search summary).
- IPCC technical BECCS potential by 2050: 0.5-11.3 GtCO2/yr; some scenarios assume up to 16 GtCO2/yr of BECCS in 2050, above that potential. VERIFIED [S] (Zero Carbon Analytics, link above).
- Land-based CDR (A/R, soil carbon) also large in AR6 pathways; the nature-side worker covers it.

### State of CDR

- 2nd edition (June 2024): in "more sustainable" scenarios, CDR is 7-9 GtCO2/yr in 2050. Current CDR about 2 GtCO2/yr, almost all conventional (forests). Novel CDR 1.3 MtCO2/yr (0.0013 Gt), less than 0.1% of total. VERIFIED [S] (Oxford news, https://ox.ac.uk/news/2024-06-05-new-report-states-7-9-billion-tonnes-co2-must-be-sustainably-removed-year-hit; IIASA, https://iiasa.ac.at/news/jun-2024/2024-state-of-cdr-report-scaling-up-co2-removal-to-meet-paris-targets).
- 2nd edition: novel CDR must grow about 30-fold by 2030 and about 1,300-fold by 2050 (from 2024 levels) in the scenarios that meet Paris. UNVERIFIED [M] — I did not find this in a snippet.
- 3rd edition (June 2026): current CDR about 2.2 GtCO2/yr (2026); about 8.75 GtCO2/yr needed by 2050 for a chance of 1.5 °C by 2100 (Carbon Brief summary). National plans fall short of 1.5 °C-compatible pathways by more than 5 GtCO2/yr by 2050. Novel CDR scales up by a factor of five between 2030 and 2035 and "accelerates to over [1] GtCO2 per year by 2050" in assessed scenarios. Novel methods are 0.1% of total removal. Only a third of countries report these techniques in NDCs. 2026-30 called "crucial". VERIFIED [S] (Carbon Brief Q&A, https://www.carbonbrief.org/qa-the-current-state-of-carbon-dioxide-removal-around-the-world/; Executive summary, https://www.stateofcdr.org/asset/The-State-of-CDR-Executive-summary_June-2026.pdf).
- 3rd edition headline: CDR "might need to scale faster than solar". VERIFIED [S] (title only; PIK, https://www.pik-potsdam.de/@@multilingual-selector/6e99e7840304422991379d366b18d797/en; Smith School, https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/news/carbon-dioxide-removal-will-need-scale-faster-solar-meet-climate-targets).
- An errata document exists for the 3rd edition; check figures against it. VERIFIED [S] (https://www.stateofcdr.org/asset/Errata-in-SoCDR-ED3_July_26-1.pdf).

### Residual emissions

- Buck et al. 2023, "Why residual emissions matter right now", Nature Climate Change 13, 351-358 (doi:10.1038/s41558-022-01592-2): analysed long-term strategies submitted to the UNFCCC. Average residual emissions in 2050 = 18% of current emissions (from press coverage, not the abstract). VERIFIED [S] (Lancaster eprint, https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/184736; Phys.org, https://phys.org/news/2023-04-overlooked-residual-emissions-critical-climate.pdf).
- Companion comment (Nature Climate Change 13, 317-319): no consistent definition of residual emissions across 50 long-term strategies. VERIFIED [S] (https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/191758).
- CALC: 18% of current GHG (~55-57 GtCO2e/yr) ≈ 10 GtCO2e/yr residual; this matches the order of the 7-9 Gt removal need. (Current GHG total is from the emissions worker; UNVERIFIED [M].)

### Removal and temperature

- TCRE best estimate 0.45 °C per 1000 GtCO2, likely range 0.27-0.63 °C. VERIFIED [S] (Open Risk Manual, https://openriskmanual.org/wiki/Transient_Climate_Response_to_Cumulative_CO2_Emissions; IPCC AR6 WG1 Figure SPM.10, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/figures/summary-for-policymakers/figure-spm-10).
- CALC: 0.1 °C ≈ 220 GtCO2 (best estimate); range ~160-370 GtCO2 over the likely TCRE range.
- UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2025 ("Off Target", 4 Nov 2025): overshoot of 1.5 °C is now near-certain at least temporarily; to reverse each 0.1 °C of overshoot, CDR must permanently remove about five years of current global CO2 emissions; the report calls CDR methods uncertain, risky and costly. "Rapid mitigation from 2025" scenario limits overshoot to about 0.3 °C (66%) and returns to 1.5 °C by 2100, with cuts of 26% by 2030 and 46% by 2035 plus scaled-up removal. NDCs fully implemented: 2.3-2.5 °C; current policies: 2.8 °C. VERIFIED [S] (UNEP, https://www.unep.org/en/publications/report/emissions-gap-report-2025; coverage IPS, https://www.ipsnews.net/2025/11/deliver-emission-cuts-or-risk-locking-the-world-into-catastrophic-warming/; Capital FM, https://capitalfm.africa/unep-warns-1-5c-overshoot-inevitable-emissions-gap-report-2025/).
- CALC: "five years of current CO2" ≈ 5 × ~40 GtCO2 = ~200 GtCO2 per 0.1 °C. This agrees with the TCRE number.
- Removal may cool slightly less per tonne than emission warms (carbon-cycle asymmetry, Zickfeld et al. 2021). UNVERIFIED [M].
- Emissions Gap Report 2026 statements: not found (not searched; may not be out). NOT VERIFIED.

## 2. Direct air capture (DAC)

### Physics and energy

- CO2 is about 0.04% (≈420 ppm) of air. UNVERIFIED [M] (science worker has the NOAA number).
- Thermodynamic minimum work to separate CO2 at 400 ppm, 300 K: ~19.5 kJ/mol = 4.43 × 10^8 J/t ≈ 0.44 GJ/t. VERIFIED [S] (Rate of Change Substack, https://rateofchange.substack.com/p/why-capturing-carbon-from-the-air). CALC: 0.44 GJ/t = 0.123 MWh/t.
- Another source gives ~0.28 MWh/t as the "thermodynamic floor" (probably includes compression or a different capture fraction). VERIFIED [S] (Energy Analytics, https://energyanalytics.org/direct-air-capture/). Disagreement noted.
- House et al. 2011 (PNAS): real systems likely need >400 kJ/mol, about 20× the minimum (CALC ≈ 9 GJ/t). VERIFIED [S] (MIT Energy Initiative, https://energy.mit.edu/publication/economic-energetic-analysis-capturing-co2-ambient-air).
- Leading players (Climeworks, Heirloom, Carbon Engineering) estimate ~2-3 MWh/t including capture and compression; solid amine sorbents regenerate at ~80-100 °C (low-grade heat). VERIFIED [S] but commentary sources (Bloomberg Línea, https://www.bloomberglinea.com/2023/02/21/cambio-climatico-la-energia-no-es-el-mayor-problema-de-captura-directa-del-aire/; LessWrong mirror, https://pf.greaterwrong.com/posts/DFyMLrcuq4ZJXwfW5/carbon-capture-is-an-energy-problem).
- Climeworks declined to publish project energy use. VERIFIED [S] (Canary Media, https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/carbon-capture/co2-removal-leader-climeworks-says-new-tech-can-halve-costs-energy-use).
- Climeworks Gen 3 (2024): structured sorbents; company says double capture per module and half energy use. VERIFIED [S] (same Canary Media article; ESG search summary).
- Liquid solvent (Carbon Engineering, Keith et al. 2018, Joule): 1 Mt/yr design, aqueous KOH + calcium caustic loop; needs 8.81 GJ natural gas, or 5.25 GJ gas + 366 kWh electricity, per tonne (CO2 delivered at 15 MPa). Levelized cost $94-232/t. VERIFIED [S] (Belfer Center, https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/process-capturing-co2-atmosphere; PDF mirror https://www.jefftk.com/keith2018.pdf). CALC: 8.81 GJ ≈ 2.45 MWh/t, mostly high-temperature heat (calciner ~900 °C, UNVERIFIED [M]).
- Electrochemical DAC (e.g., pH-swing, Verdox, Mission Zero): electricity-only; early stage. UNVERIFIED [M]. Holocene (amino acids, low-temperature, liquid) bought by Occidental in April 2025; 2024 pilot ~10 t/yr; Google contract 100,000 t at $100/t (Sept 2024). VERIFIED [S] (TechCrunch, https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/17/carbon-removal-startup-holocene-bought-by-oil-and-gas-giant-occidental; ESG News, https://esgnews.com/oxy-buys-dac-startup-holocene-following-100-ton-carbon-removal-deal-with-google/).
- Occidental bought Carbon Engineering in 2023 for about $1.1 billion. VERIFIED [S] (same TechCrunch search result).

### Energy and land for 1 GtCO2/yr

- Belfer Center: 1 Gt DACCS needs 1,400-4,200 TWh/yr of low-carbon energy. VERIFIED [S] (https://www.belfercenter.org/publication/prospects-direct-air-carbon-capture-and-storage-costs-scale-and-funding).
- MIT commentary: 1-7 km² for a 1 Mt/yr DAC facility (CALC: 1,000-7,000 km² per Gt, plant only, not energy supply). VERIFIED [S] (MIT CS3, https://cs3.mit.edu/publication/18264/file/Getting%20real%20about%20capturing%20carbon%20from%20the%20air%20%28Commentary%29).
- CALC: 1.5-2.5 MWh/t × 1 Gt = 1,500-2,500 TWh/yr; world electricity generation ~30,000 TWh/yr (UNVERIFIED [M]) → ~5-8% of world electricity per Gt, or ~5% of world final energy use at the high end.

### Costs

- Sievert et al. 2024 (Joule, doi:10.1016/j.joule.2024.02.005, ETH Zurich): 2050 cost $230-540/t (about twice earlier estimates). At 1 Gt/yr cumulative capacity, net removal cost $226-835/t, central ~$340/t. VERIFIED [S] (ScienceDaily, https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/03/240304135808.htm; Google Groups abstract mirror, https://groups.google.com/g/CarbonDioxideRemoval/c/E0d-mdX17xw; Milkywire, https://milkywire.com/articles/direct-air-capture-is-cheaper-than-you-think).
- Counterpoint: DAC companies' own estimates average $137/t (median $110) at Mt scale — self-reported, not peer-reviewed. VERIFIED [S] (Milkywire, link above).
- Climeworks: Gen 3 to cut costs by $250-350/t captured, reaching $400-600/t net removal by 2030. Some outlets misreport "$250-350/t by 2030" as the target price. Earlier aim was $100/t by 2030. Current cost near $1,000/t. VERIFIED [S] (EnkiAI, https://enkiai.com/climeworks-2025-dac-growth-risk-market-analysis; Swissinfo, https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/climate-solutions/why-swiss-carbon-removal-start-up-climeworks-faces-serious-headwinds/89386618; cleanthinking.de, https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1069227). Note the user's brief said "$250-350 by 2030"; the snippets support "$250-350 reduction, $400-600 total". Check Climeworks' June 2024 Gen 3 release.
- CDR.fyi × OPIS 2026 survey: DACCS supplier breakeven $670/t in 2025, $341/t average for 2030 (wide spread). VERIFIED [S] (https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/cdr-pricing-survey-may-2026; OPIS, https://www.opis.com/blog/pricing-gap-in-durable-carbon-dioxide-removal-revealed-in-new-survey/).

### US policy

- 45Q in the 2025 budget law ("One Big Beautiful Bill Act", July 2025): DAC with dedicated geologic storage stays at $180/t; CO2 used for EOR or utilisation now gets the same credit as dedicated storage (DAC utilisation rose from $130 to $180/t; point source $60 to $85/t). Transferability kept; foreign-entity limits added. Critics call parity a subsidy for oil extraction. VERIFIED [S] (Global CCS Institute, https://www.globalccsinstitute.com/u-s-preserves-and-increases-45q-credit-in-one-big-beautiful-bill-act/; Enverus, https://www.enverus.com/newsroom/making-enhanced-oil-recovery-great-again-obba-45q-tax-credit-shift-cuts-ep-costs-by-40/; Payne Institute, https://payneinstitute.mines.edu/keeping-up-with-carbon-key-changes-for-45q-tax-credits-under-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-and-possible-impacts/). Construction-start deadline (before 2033) UNVERIFIED [M].
- DOE DAC Hubs: South Texas DAC Hub (Occidental) and Project Cypress (Louisiana, Heirloom/Climeworks) were awarded up to $500 million and $550 million; each received only a first $50 million tranche. They appeared on a leaked cancellation list in late 2025; DOE later said they keep funding (about $1.2 billion combined retained, reported April 2026). DOE FY2027 budget request proposes rescinding $2.3 billion of the $3.5 billion DAC Hubs program. VERIFIED [S] (PGJ, https://pgjonline.com/news/2026/april/us-doe-restores-funding-for-carbon-capture-hubs-in-texas-louisiana; E&E News, https://www.eenews.net/articles/carbon-removal-hubs-languish-as-doe-audits-drag-on/; Latitude Media, https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/can-domestic-dac-survive-without-the-help-of-doe/).

## 3. BECCS

- Mechanism: grow biomass (takes CO2 from air), burn or ferment it for energy, capture the biogenic CO2 and store it geologically. UNVERIFIED [M] (standard).
- Current scale: novel CDR total was 1.3 Mt/yr in the 2024 State of CDR (BECCS included). VERIFIED [S] (see section 1). The "~2 Mt/yr, mostly ethanol" figure: NOT VERIFIED.
- ADM Decatur, Illinois (ethanol): ~1 Mt/yr design, injecting since 2017; cumulative > 3.5-3.9 Mt (ADM figures conflict). VERIFIED [S] (https://carbonstorage.io/facilities/illinois-industrial-ccs; https://www.adm.com/globalassets/standalone-pages/carbon-capture-and-storage/adm-ccs-overview-aug-2023.pdf).
- ADM Columbus, Nebraska (with Tallgrass, started 2025): > 800,000 t/yr removal capacity; ADM entering the voluntary removal market (Sept 2026). VERIFIED [S] (Business Wire, https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260921029732/en/ADM-Plans-Entry-into-Voluntary-Carbon-Market-with-Over-800000-Tons-of-Annual-Removal-Capacity; DTN, https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/AG/news/article/2026/09/21/adm-enter-co2-removal-market-credits).
- Drax (UK): planning consent for BECCS on two units (Jan 2024); paused £2 billion BECCS investment pending government support; new biomass subsidy deal to March 2031 with halved support; E&E News (9 Apr 2026): "Energy giant Drax pulls out of UK climate plan" — government did not lock Drax into a BECCS agreement and Drax "walked away". Details not read. VERIFIED [S] (E&E News, https://www.eenews.net/articles/energy-giant-drax-pulls-out-of-uk-climate-plan/; Carbon Pulse, https://carbon-pulse.com/367345/; ThePrint/Reuters, https://theprint.in/environment/britains-drax-pauses-2-5-billion-biomass-carbon-capture-plans/1461291). Status in Nov 2026: Drax UK BECCS appears off or indefinitely paused — check.
- Drax criticism: wood pellets from forests; NRDC and DeSmog call it a subsidy capture. VERIFIED [S] (NRDC, https://www.nrdc.org/bio/matt-williams/united-kingdom-hands-billions-more-subsidies-drax-tree-burning-bioenergy).
- Stockholm Exergi (Värtaverket, Sweden): FID March 2025; groundbreaking 12 June 2025; up to 800,000 t/yr bioCO2; fully operational 2028. Microsoft contract 3.33 Mt (2024) expanded to 5.08 Mt over 10 years (2025). EIB loan €260 million; EU Innovation Fund; winner of Sweden's first BECCS reverse auction. Storage via Northern Lights (up to 900,000 t/yr agreement). VERIFIED [S] (EIB, https://www.eib.org/en/press/all/2025-172-eib-finances-ground-breaking-carbon-capture-plant-in-stockholm; Biofuels Digest, https://biofuelsdigest.com/stockholm-exergi-makes-fid-on-worlds-first-large-scale-beccs-project/; ESG Today, https://www.esgtoday.com/?p=20283).
- Land: IPCC SR1.5 pathways need 93 (P2), 283 (P3) and 724 (P4) million ha of BECCS feedstock land in 2050. SRCCL: models use 109-990 Mha, most ~380-700 Mha. ## 5. Enhanced rock weathering (ERW)

- Mechanism: spread crushed silicate rock (basalt) on fields; it reacts with CO2 dissolved in soil water to form bicarbonate, which drains to rivers and ocean. UNVERIFIED [M] (standard).
- Beerling et al. 2020 (Nature): China, India, USA and Brazil could deliver 0.5-2 GtCO2/yr at ~$80-180/t. VERIFIED [S] (https://remineralize.org/?p=12842; https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/442331/).
- Beerling et al. 2025 (Nature, US only): 0.16-0.30 GtCO2/yr by 2050, 0.25-0.49 by 2070; costs high at first, down to ~$100-150/t by 2050; main uncertainty: basalt mineralogy. VERIFIED [S] (https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v638y2025i8050d10.1038_s41586-024-08429-2.html; Heriot-Watt, https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/transforming-us-agriculture-for-carbon-removal-with-enhanced-weat/).
- Corn Belt 4-year trial (Beerling et al. 2024, PNAS): reproducible removal with higher soil fertility and crop yield. VERIFIED [S] (https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/490716/). Yield gain size (~12-16% maize/soy): UNVERIFIED [M].
- Counter-evidence: Australian basalt trial — weathering seen, no crop yield increase; low in-field CDR where soil pH is well below 6.4. Minnesota 3-year trial: CDR evidence for steel slag but not basalt. Measuring field weathering "perhaps the most difficult part"; cation-loss proxies rely on assumptions (charge balance). VERIFIED [S] (JCU, https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/84066/1/84066.pdf; Biogeosciences 2026, https://bg.copernicus.org/articles/23/7727/2026/; Frontiers 2025, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fclim.2025.1657058).
- Losses downstream (soil carbonate formation, river/ocean degassing) reduce net removal: UNVERIFIED [M].

## 9. Cost and scale reality check

- World GDP 2025 ≈ $113.8-117.2 trillion (IMF WEO April/October 2025 editions, via secondary sites). VERIFIED [S] (Statistics Times, https://www.statisticstimes.com/m/economy/world-gdp.php; BusinessDay, https://businessday.ng/news/article/top-10-largest-economies-in-the-115tn-world-of-2025/).
- CALC, using $117 trillion:

| Removal | $100/t | $300/t | $600/t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Gt/yr | $100 bn (0.09% of GDP) | $300 bn (0.26%) | $600 bn (0.51%) |
| 10 Gt/yr | $1.0 tn (0.85%) | $3.0 tn (2.6%) | $6.0 tn (5.1%) |

- Comparison: Sievert's central $340/t at 1 Gt → ~$340 bn/yr. Cheap mitigation options in AR6 WG3: about half of 2030 potential at < $20/tCO2e, and options < $100/t could halve 2019 emissions by 2030. UNVERIFIED [M] (AR6 WG3 SPM C.12). So most avoidance is far cheaper per tonne than engineered removal; removal is for residuals and overshoot.
- Learning rates: DAC learning rate is unknown (too few data points — Frontier). Chemical process plants historically ~10-15% per doubling, solar ~20%. DAC is expected to learn slower than small modular technologies. VERIFIED [S] (Frontier, https://frontierclimate.com/pathway/direct-air-capture; AccumulationZone, https://accumulationzone.substack.com/p/how-smarter-policy-could-make-or; arXiv 2411.15369 critique by Eisenberger and Realff, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15369).
- For: sorbent DAC is modular (containers, many identical units), so it may learn more like mass-produced goods (Climeworks, Heirloom argument). Against: Malhotra & Schmidt (Joule 2020) — complex, design-intensive, customised technologies learn slowly; DAC sits between modular and lumpy; energy and sorbent costs set floors; thermodynamic minimum is small, but real systems run at ~5-10% second-law efficiency. Malhotra & Schmidt: UNVERIFIED [M] (search did not reach the paper).
- Mitigation deterrence: McLaren et al. 2019 (Frontiers in Climate, doi:10.3389/fclim.2019.00004) — set separate targets, accounting and incentives for emissions reduction and for negative emissions, because promised future removals (discounted in models) can substitute for near-term cuts; mechanisms: temporal trade-offs, excessive offsetting, lock-in. VERIFIED [S] (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/climate/articles/10.3389/fclim.2019.00004/full; Carbon Brief guest post, https://carbonbrief.org/guest-post-the-problem-with-net-zero-emissions-targets).
- Counter-argument: some removal is unavoidable for residual emissions (agriculture, aviation, cement process) and for overshoot reversal; IPCC says CDR is unavoidable for net zero; Buck et al. find residual emissions ~18% of today in national plans; the EU adopted a separate removals target concept in its 2040 proposal. IPCC "unavoidable": UNVERIFIED [M] wording; Buck VERIFIED [S] (section 1); EU separate target UNVERIFIED [M].
- Oxy "net-zero oil" is the concrete example critics give of deterrence. VERIFIED [S] (section 2).

## 10. Point-source CCS vs removal

- IPCC AR6 glossary: CDR = "anthropogenic activities removing CO2 from the atmosphere and durably storing it in geological, terrestrial, or ocean reservoirs". VERIFIED [S] quoted in search summary (State of CDR Ed3 Ch1, https://www.stateofcdr.org/asset/SoCDR_ED3_Chpater-1_June-2026.pdf).
- State of CDR principle 1: the CO2 captured must come from the atmosphere, not fossil sources; fossil point-source capture is "fossil CCS", not CDR. Global CCS Institute: CCS counts as CDR only if the whole chain gives a net removal from the atmosphere. Capture rate is always < 100%. VERIFIED [S] (SoCDR Ed3 Ch1 link above; Global CCS Institute, https://www.globalccsinstitute.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Carbon-Management-and-the-Paris-Agreement-Fact-Sheet-Global-CCS-Institute.pdf).
- Reasoning: capturing a gas plant's CO2 at 90% only cuts that plant's new emissions by 90% (minus upstream methane and energy penalty); atmospheric CO2 still rises. It is mitigation, not removal. BECCS and DACCS use the same capture-and-storage chain but the carbon came from the air. CALC/standard reasoning.
- Storage in use today ~49 Mt/yr, mostly linked to EOR (Gidden et al. coverage). VERIFIED [S] (section 7).
