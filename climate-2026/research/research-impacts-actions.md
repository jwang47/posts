Highly vulnerable regions named: East, Central and West Africa, South Asia, Central and South America, small island states, the Arctic.

## 2. Health

- **Lancet Countdown 2025** (published 29 Oct 2025): heat-related deaths averaged **546,000 per year in 2012–2021**; heat mortality per 100,000 rose 23% since the 1990s (the absolute number rose about 63%). The average person had **16 extra days of dangerous heat in 2024** that would not happen without climate change. **640 billion potential labour hours lost** to heat in 2024, worth about **US$1.09 trillion**. Record **154,000 deaths from wildfire-smoke PM2.5 in 2024**. 12 of 20 health-threat indicators at record levels. https://lancetcountdown.org/2025-report/ ; https://www.who.int/news/item/29-10-2025-climate-inaction-is-claiming-millions-of-lives-every-year--warns-new-lancet-countdown-report (VERIFIED, snippet)
- Lancet Countdown 2025: **2.52 million deaths from fossil-fuel-derived outdoor air pollution in 2022**; 2.3 million deaths from dirty-fuel household air pollution in 2022. Reduced coal burning prevented about **160,000 premature deaths per year** (2010–2022). Net fossil fuel subsidies **US$956 billion in 2023** (peak $1.4 trillion in 2022); 15 of 87 countries spent more on net fossil subsidies than on health. https://lancetcountdown.org/2025-report/ ; https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/newsevents/news/2025/climate-change-inaction-costs-millions-lives-each-year-report-warns (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **Lelieveld et al., BMJ, 29 Nov 2023**: **5.13 million (3.63–6.32 million) excess deaths per year** from ambient air pollution from fossil fuel use; total air pollution deaths 8.34 million per year. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/11/231130113241.htm ; https://www.unimedizin-mainz.de/en/newsroom/news/news-releases/newsdetail/article/phasing-out-fossil-fuels-could-save-millions-of-lives.html (VERIFIED, snippet). The two estimates (2.5 vs 5.1 million) differ by method (exposure-response function, attribution approach). State both. The co-benefit is local and near-term, unlike most climate benefits.
- **WHO**: climate change is expected to cause about **250,000 additional deaths per year between 2030 and 2050** (38,000 heat in elderly, 48,000 diarrhoea, 60,000 malaria, 95,000 child undernutrition). Note: this 2014 estimate is now old; the Lancet heat figure alone (546,000/yr, all heat deaths, not only the climate-attributable part) is a different metric.

## 5. "Catastrophic" vs "bad": the range of expert views

- **Kemp et al., "Climate Endgame", PNAS, Aug 2022**: argues that catastrophic outcomes (societal collapse, even extinction) are "dangerously underexplored"; calls for research on warming of 3 °C+, cascading risks and interactions; "We know least about the scenarios that matter most." It does not estimate probabilities. https://www.cam.ac.uk/stories/climateendgame ; https://publications.pik-potsdam.de/pubman/item/item_27913_4 (VERIFIED, snippet). UNVERIFIED (memory): paper says 3 °C by 2100 implies ~2 billion people in areas with mean annual temperature ≥29 °C (Xu et al. 2020 niche).
- **Toby Ord, The Precipice (2020)**: existential risk from climate change ~**1 in 1,000 this century** (vs total ~1 in 6, mostly from AI and engineered pandemics). He later noted (Precipice Revisited, 2024) that narrower climate sensitivity (AR6 likely 2.5–4 °C) lowers the tail. https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/the-precipice-existential-risk-and-the-future-of-humanity/ ; https://www.tobyord.com/writing/the-precipice-revisited (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **80,000 Hours climate profile**: climate change "seems more than 10 times less likely to cause extinction than nuclear war or pandemics", but still a major problem and relatively tractable. https://80000hours.org/problem-profiles/climate-change/ (VERIFIED, snippet)
- Current-policy warming: UNEP Emissions Gap 2025 or Climate Action Tracker give about 2.3–2.8 °C by 2100 (UNVERIFIED here; another worker covers this).
- Fair summary: mainstream assessments (IPCC) describe 3 °C+ as severe, with large mortality, food, water and ecosystem impacts that fall hardest on poor regions, and rising tipping-point risk; human extinction from climate alone is judged very unlikely by most researchers who have estimated it, but the high-impact tail (high sensitivity, tipping cascades, societal collapse) is under-studied (Kemp). Each tenth of a degree matters; no single threshold marks a cliff.
- **Misperceptions**:
  - Doomism: Hickman et al. 2021: **56%** of 16–25 year-olds in 10 countries agreed "humanity is doomed"; **75%** "the future is frightening"; ~4 in 10 hesitant to have children. https://grist.org/article/climate-anxiety-study-young-people-think-humanity-is-doomed/ (VERIFIED, snippet). Hannah Ritchie argues the "doomed" framing is not supported by evidence and that wording is open to interpretation. https://hannahritchie.substack.com/p/young-climate-anxiety (VERIFIED, snippet)
  - Dismissal and underestimation of others: see Andre et al. 2024 perception gap (section 12). Sparkman et al., Nat. Commun. 2022: Americans underestimate support for climate policies by nearly half. https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022NatCo..13.4779S (VERIFIED, snippet, title only)

## 6. Snow and skiing: Japan / Hokkaido

- **Gottlieb & Mankin, Nature, Jan 2024** ("Evidence of human influence on Northern Hemisphere snow loss", doi 10.1038/s41586-023-06794-y): 169 river basins, 1981–2020; human influence confidently detected in 31 basins. ~80% of NH snowpack (cold, high latitude/altitude) had minimal losses; biggest declines **10–20% per decade** in SW and NE US, Central and Eastern Europe. Non-linear "snow-loss cliff": losses accelerate once average winter temperature exceeds about **−8 °C (17 °F)**. https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/01/240110120210.htm ; https://www.drought.gov/news/climate-change-behind-sharp-drop-snowpack-1980s-2024-01-24 (VERIFIED, snippet). Implication: very cold places (much of inland Hokkaido) sit on the safe side of the cliff for now; marginal areas (Honshu low elevations) do not.
- **JMA/MEXT "Climate Change in Japan 2025"** (Mar 2025): since 1962 annual maximum snow depth has decreased in all Sea of Japan side regions; under **4 °C (RCP8.5)**, end-of-century annual maximum snow depth and annual snowfall **decrease significantly nationwide (high confidence)**; under 2 °C (RCP2.6) the trend for Hokkaido is unclear. Extreme heavy snowfall may increase in some inland/cold areas even as mean snowfall falls (medium confidence); once-in-10-year heavy snowfall intensifies in inland high-altitude central Japan. https://www.data.jma.go.jp/cpdinfo/ccj/2025/pdf/cc2025_shousai_6.pdf ; https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/Climate_Change_In_Japan_2025.pdf (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **Climate Change in Japan 2020** magnitudes: annual max snow depth and snowfall **about −30% at 2 °C (except Hokkaido and some areas), about −70% at 4 °C (except some areas of Hokkaido)**. https://www.data.jma.go.jp/cpdinfo/ccj/2020/pdf/cc2020_gaiyo_en.pdf (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **Sapporo Regional HQ (JMA), 2017**: Hokkaido annual max snow depth about **−44%** by end of century (Pacific side ~−50%; smaller declines inland) under the high scenario. https://www.data.jma.go.jp/sapporo/bosai/publication/kiko/kikohenka/pdf/report.pdf (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **Kawase et al. 2016** (Climatic Change, d4PDF): extremely heavy daily snowfall could increase in inland Sea of Japan side of central Japan under warming even while total snowfall falls. https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/10.1007/s10584-016-1781-3 (VERIFIED, snippet). Hokkaido-specific: Inatsu et al. (2021, d4PDF) find more heavy snowfall for one weather pattern; Inatsu & Oonishi (JpGU 2024): warming increases snowfall over **western and central Hokkaido** and decreases it on the southern coast. https://pub.confit.atlas.jp/en/event/jpgu2024/presentation/AAS07-P04 (VERIFIED, snippet). Increased risk of heavy wet snowfall over the northern Japan Alps and Hokkaido. (VERIFIED, snippet; Kawase et al. ~2020, "Impacts of climate change on heavy wet snowfall in Japan")
- Attribution: Japanese team: warming gave Hokkaido up to 20% more snow than average in Dec 2021. https://japantimes.co.jp/environment/2024/12/18/climate-change/sapporo-snowfall-climate-change (VERIFIED, snippet). JMA MRI (Mar 2026): warming likely raised snowfall ~7% in colder Sea of Japan regions (Niigata and north). https://snowbrains.com/new-study-finds-climate-change-increased-snowfall-in-northern-japan-by-7/ (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **Ski areas**: Yoshizawa & Katsuyama (J. Japanese Society of Snow and Ice 88(4), ~2026): 349 Japanese ski areas, d4PDF 5 km + SNOWPACK. **+4 °C: only 7 fully operable on natural snow, 26 partly. +2 °C: 53 fully, 108 partly.** Hokkaido and high Nagano keep a relative advantage; under +4 °C most areas get <10 fresh-snow days a season. https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/seppyo/88/4/88_295/_article/-char/en (VERIFIED, snippet)
- Takeaway for a Hokkaido skier: near term (1.5–2 °C), inland Hokkaido snowfall is roughly stable and heavy-snow events may even intensify; powder quality and season length at lower elevations and on the Pacific side decline; at 4 °C, large declines nearly everywhere.

## 9. "Carbon footprint" framing and individual vs systemic

- BP's ad agency **Ogilvy & Mather** built the "beyond petroleum" campaign; **BP launched a personal carbon footprint calculator in 2004**; ~278,000 people used it that year (BP via AdWeek, cited by DeSmog); Google search interest in the term was near zero before. https://grist.org/energy/footprint-fantasy/ ; https://www.desmog.com/ogilvy/ ; https://fullsite.motherjones.com/?p=862397 (VERIFIED, snippet). Nuance: the "ecological footprint" concept (Wackernagel & Rees, 1990s) predates BP; BP popularised the personal carbon version. Critics call it a "blame the consumer" tactic.
- The individual vs systemic debate: most researchers now treat it as a false choice. Individual actions matter (a) directly for high emitters, (b) as signals that shift norms and markets, and (c) through civic action that changes systems. (Interpretive; supported by Stern 2017 above.)
- **Social contagion, solar**: Bollinger & Gillingham, Marketing Science 31(6):900, 2012: one more installation in a zip code raises the probability of an additional adoption by **0.78 percentage points** (at the average zip-code size); stronger at street level. https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ormksc:v:31:y:2012:i:6:p:900-912 (VERIFIED, snippet)
- **Social contagion, EVs**: Tebbe (Sweden working paper): neighbours', coworkers' and family members' EV purchases raise own adoption; peer effects' cumulative effect = 3.8% of average individual emissions. https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f179679/f179679.pdf (VERIFIED, snippet). Generation180 (advocacy, 2023, 11 US metros): strong peer-effect signal. https://generation180.org/resource/your-influence-matters-peer-influence-and-electric-vehicle-adoption (VERIFIED, snippet)
- Also relevant (UNVERIFIED, memory): Westlake 2018 survey (UK): about half of respondents who knew someone who gave up flying for climate said they flew less as a result; Sparkman & Walton 2017 dynamic norms and meat consumption.

## 12. Public opinion and climate anxiety

- **Andre, Boneva, Chopra & Falk, Nature Climate Change 14:253–259, 2024**: ~130,000 people in 125 countries (2021–22 Gallup survey). **89% demand intensified political action; 86% endorse pro-climate social norms; 69% willing to contribute 1% of personal income**.
