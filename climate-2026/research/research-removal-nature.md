### Why existing sinks are not "removal"
- **Allen et al. 2024/2025, Nature 638, 343-350, "Geological Net Zero and the need for disaggregated accounting for carbon sinks":** many accounting systems count "passive" uptake (for example CO2 fertilisation) as removals; then nominal net zero would not halt warming. Splitting active from passive uptake needs a modelled counterfactual. Proposal: every tonne of fossil CO2 balanced by one tonne stored geologically ("geological net zero"); claimed removals should be additional to passive uptake. https://www.inet.ox.ac.uk/publications/geological-net-zero-and-the-need-for-disaggregated-accounting-for-carbon-sinks ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v638y2025i8050d10.1038_s41586-024-08326-8.html ; https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/scientists-argue-natural-forests-should-not-count-towards-climate-goals. VERIFIED (snippet)
- **The inventory gap (Grassi et al. 2023, ESSD 15, 1093):** bookkeeping models give land use a net SOURCE of 4.8 GtCO2/yr (2000-2020); national inventories give a net SINK of 1.9 GtCO2/yr. Gap 6.7 GtCO2/yr, mostly forest land (3.5). Cause: countries count the indirect sink (CO2 fertilisation etc.) on "managed" land as anthropogenic; models do not. Adding the model sink in managed forests (-6.4 GtCO2/yr) leaves a residual gap of 0.3. https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/18687/ ; https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/18687/1/essd-15-1093-2023.pdf. VERIFIED (snippet)
- My reading: national "removals" in inventories include part of the natural sink. Those tonnes would happen anyway, so they are not deliberate removal in the Allen sense.

VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Mo et al. 2023:** 61% of the natural forest carbon potential is in existing forests (protection and recovery), not new planting. (Section 3.) VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Proforestation (Moomaw et al. 2019, Frontiers in Forests and Global Change):** let existing forests grow to their ecological potential; faster and cheaper than A/R in the next decades because young trees store little carbon. US temperate and boreal forests offset 11% of US net emissions. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/forests-and-global-change/articles/10.3389/ffgc.2019.00027/pdf. VERIFIED (snippet)

From memory: intact moist tropical forest holds about 150-200 tC/ha above ground (550-730 tCO2/ha); clearing releases most of it in a few years; regrowth at about 3 tC/ha/yr (Poorter) needs about 66 years to recover 90% of biomass. ## 6. Soil carbon and biochar

### Soil carbon
- **4 per 1000 initiative:** launched by France at COP21 (2015), Lima-Paris Action Agenda. Idea: raise soil carbon stocks 0.4%/yr to offset a large share of fossil CO2. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6889108 ; https://rothamsted.ac.uk/news/soil-fails-climate-challenge. VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Poulton et al. 2018, Global Change Biology (gcb.14066):** 16 long-term Rothamsted experiments, 114 comparisons over 7-157 years. 65% of cases gave >7 per mille/yr in 0-23 cm (about 4 per mille in 0-40 cm), but mostly with extreme measures: manure rates beyond EU limits (18 and 43 per mille/yr in the first 20 years), taking land out of farming, or at least 3 years in 5-6 under grass. "Severe limitations" over large areas; promote soil carbon for soil quality, not as a major climate tool. Gains stop at a new equilibrium. https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/84663 ; https://phys.org/news/2018-02-soil-halt-climate.html. VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Bossio et al. 2020, Nature Sustainability 3:391:** soil carbon is 25% of the 23.8 GtCO2e/yr natural climate solution potential (about 5.5 GtCO2e/yr, DERIVED); 40% of that is protecting existing soil carbon, 60% rebuilding depleted stocks. https://www.conservationgateway.org/collections/climate/the-role-soil-carbon-natural-climate-solutions. VERIFIED (snippet)
- **No-till (Powlson et al. 2014, Nature Climate Change 4:678):** potential widely overstated; 69 paired comparisons; much of the gain is redistribution to the surface with less carbon deeper; shallow sampling exaggerates the effect; only a small net gain. https://repository.rothamsted.ac.uk/item/8qz04/limited-potential-of-no-till-agriculture-for-climate-change-mitigation ; https://www.gwct.org.uk/media/841523/Powlson-2014-Limited-potential-for-no-till.pdf. VERIFIED (snippet). N2O offset claim: UNVERIFIED.
- **Cover crops (Poeplau & Don 2015):** about 0.32 MgC/ha/yr (about 1.2 tCO2/ha/yr, DERIVED); global 0.12 GtC/yr (about 0.44 GtCO2/yr, DERIVED) if on 25% of cropland (400 Mha); subsoil effect uncertain. Disputed by Chaplot & Smith 2023; rebuttal cites five meta-analyses with +11.6% mean SOC. https://typeset.io/papers/carbon-sequestration-in-agricultural-soils-via-cultivation-a2qw4xkk2j ; https://www.openagrar.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/openagrar_derivate_00053391/dn066490.pdf. VERIFIED (snippet), partly secondary.
- **Reversibility:** soil carbon gains are lost again if practice stops; erosion-lost carbon gives no benefit; saturation in about 20-100 years. https://rothamsted.ac.uk/news/soil-fails-climate-challenge (equilibrium point VERIFIED snippet); 20-100 year saturation seen only in a mailing list (https://www.mail-archive.com/geoengineering@googlegroups.com/msg14187.html): UNVERIFIED. IPCC default 20-year transition period: UNVERIFIED, memory.

### Biochar
- **Potential:** IPCC AR6 WGIII Ch.12 summary table: 0.3-6.6 GtCO2/yr at $10-345/tCO2 (reproduced in https://zerocarbon-analytics.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/IPCC-Working-Group-III-report_-The-land-sector-and-climate-mitigation-Appendix-1.pdf, VERIFIED snippet, secondary; check Ch.12 table). Other expert assessments: 0.5-2 GtCO2/yr by 2050 at $30-120/t; 1.8-4.8 GtCO2/yr at $18-166/t, about half needing purpose-grown biomass. https://www.american.edu/sis/centers/carbon-removal/fact-sheet-biochar.cfm. VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Permanence:** debate. A 2025 GCB Bioenergy commentary (Schmidt, Abiven, Woolf et al.) rejects the "inertinite" claim that biochar with reflectance Ro >= 2% is 100% persistent for millennia; recommends policy use centuries. Puro.earth cross-registry workshop (Oct 2025): no strong consensus on 1,000-year persistence; "several centuries" is the conservative label. European Biochar Certificate assumes 0.3%/yr decay. https://biochartoday.com/news/should-policy-define-biochar-persistence-over-centuries-rather-than-millennia/ ; https://puro.earth/our-blog/352-biochar-permanence-key-learnings-from-the-cross-standard-workshop ; https://www.american.edu/sis/centers/carbon-removal/fact-sheet-biochar.cfm (EBC from senken.io snippet). VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Deliveries:** biochar is "the undisputed delivery leader" among CDR methods (CDR.fyi snapshot, Sep 2025, https://www.cdr.fyi/blog/biochar-carbon-removal-market-snapshot-2025, VERIFIED snippet). Shares: 86-94% of delivered tonnes in 2023-24 and >90% of durable credits delivered in 2025 (secondary sources citing CDR.fyi): UNVERIFIED. Durable CDR deliveries about 1 Mt in 2025, 521 buyers; durable purchases >$11 billion in 2025 vs $3.34 billion in 2024 (secondary, https://decarbonfuse.com/posts/north-america-s-carbon-removal-year-in-review-the-deals-policies-and-milestones-that-shaped-2025 ; https://groups.google.com/g/CarbonDioxideRemoval/c/mtsET52TNjc): UNVERIFIED against CDR.fyi. Biochar contracted 3.04 Mt 2022-mid 2025: UNVERIFIED (secondary).
- **Biochar prices:** S&P Global: US biochar credits about $150/tCO2e (Oct 2025); Nasdaq CORCCHAR index $125-145 per CORC in 2025; CDR.fyi/OPIS survey: biochar breakeven $143/t, sellers need about $187, buyers call $155 expensive; "$164 average (H1 2025)" attributed to CDR.fyi in secondary sources. https://carboncredits.com/?p=39628 ; https://cdr.fyi/blog/cdr-pricing-survey-jan-2025 ; https://www.opis.com/blog/pricing-gap-in-durable-carbon-dioxide-removal-revealed-in-new-survey/. VERIFIED (snippet), mostly secondary.

## 7. Blue carbon, peatlands, ocean biology

- **Blue carbon restoration potential, estimates disagree:** Macreadie et al. 2021: 841 MtCO2e/yr at full potential on about 30 Mha (17.5-41.6), 57% seagrass, 37% mangrove, about 3% of fossil emissions (https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/D875C9D7C90D0A5D8ED96F62D1180520/S275472052400009Xa.pdf/restoring-blue-carbon-ecosystems.pdf). Oxford review: about 400 MtCO2e/yr including kelp (https://www.geog.ox.ac.uk/research/blue-carbon-restoration-vcms). Hoegh-Guldberg et al. 2023 via Ocean Visions: mangroves 0.028-0.172, salt marsh 0.004-0.015 GtCO2e/yr; seagrass least understood (https://www2.oceanvisions.org/roadmaps/remove/mcdr/blue-carbon-restoration-and-carbon-sequestration/coastal-vegetated-ecosystems/). McKinsey 2022: 0.4-1.2 GtCO2/yr by 2050 including avoided emissions from protection. Restoration so far small-scale, mixed success. VERIFIED (snippet). Range for removal: about 0.03-0.84 GtCO2e/yr.
- **Co-benefits:** coastal protection, fisheries nurseries, biodiversity. UNVERIFIED (memory; general knowledge, not searched).
- **Peatlands, mostly avoided emissions:** drained peatlands emit about 2 GtCO2/yr (about 4-5% of anthropogenic GHG) on 0.3% of land (Ramsar; Nature Comms 2020); Defra synthesis 1.2-1.9 GtCO2e/yr. Main benefit of rewetting is avoided CO2; methane rises, but CH4 forcing does not undo the benefit (Gunther et al. 2020, Nature Communications). https://www.ramsar.org/sites/default/files/documents/library/rpb5_restoring_drained_peatlands_e.pdf ; https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41467-020-15499-z ; https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3160087/1/Defra_lowland_peat_synthesis_v8.pdf. VERIFIED (snippet). Author name Gunther: UNVERIFIED.
- **Iron fertilisation:** at least 16 open-ocean experiments; some (2004 Southern Ocean EIFEX) showed at least half of bloom carbon sank below 1,000 m; many showed little or undetectable export; N2O/CH4 side effects. London Convention/Protocol 2008 resolution: large-scale fertilisation not justified, only legitimate research; 2013 amendment to prohibit marine geoengineering except research. https://www.geoengineeringmonitor.org/technologies/ocean-fertilisation ; https://tos.org/oceanography/assets/docs/22-3_strong.pdf ; https://www.eco-business.com/news/fertilizing-oceans-with-iron-dust. VERIFIED (snippet), partly advocacy sources. 2013 amendment not yet in force (too few ratifications): UNVERIFIED, memory. 2012 Haida Gwaii rogue dumping: UNVERIFIED, memory.
- **Running Tide:** shut down June 2024; CEO cited lack of demand and financing in the voluntary market. Sold early marine CDR credits to Microsoft, Shopify, Stripe (25 customers). Icelandic reporting: it sank about 19,000 t of Canadian wood chips (with lime-kiln dust) off Iceland rather than mainly kelp. https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/environment-sustainability/carbon-removal-company-running-tide-shuts-down-operations ; https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/carbon-removal/under-the-sea-running-tides-ill-fated-adventure-in-ocean-carbon-removal ; https://www.restore.org/maineenvironews/2024/6/18/scientists-criticize-practices-of-shuttered-portland-carbon-capture-company. VERIFIED (snippet)
- **Seaweed sinking verification:** CarbonPlan rates ocean biomass sinking (no harvest) VCL 1-2: very significant uncertainty (https://carbonplan.org/research/cdr-verification/ocean-biomass-sinking-no-harvest). Bauer et al. 2026 (Communications Earth & Environment): sunk kelp degrades fast in situ, limiting sequestration and altering the seafloor (https://carbondioxide-removal.eu/2026/03/20/bauer-et-al-2026-in-situ-deep-ocean-monitoring-reveals-rapid-kelp-degradation-limits-marine-biomass-based-carbon-sequestration-potential-and-alters-benthic-ecosystems/). Model preprint 2025: nutrient trapping cuts phytoplankton; seafloor oxygen loss (https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2025/egusphere-2025-5360/egusphere-2025-5360-manuscript-version3.pdf). VERIFIED (snippet)

## 8. Durability and accounting

- **Like for like:** fossil CO2 affects climate for millennia, so storage of decades to a century does not neutralise it. Oxford Offsetting Principles (2020, revised Feb 2024): shift to removals, then to removals with durable storage and low reversal risk, by the net-zero date; never enough high-quality removals to offset avoidable fossil emissions like for like, so reserve them for residual emissions. https://www.ox.ac.uk/news/2024-02-28-oxford-researchers-launch-updated-carbon-offsetting-principles ; https://www.smithschool.ox.ac.uk/news/oxford-report-calls-major-course-correction-carbon-offsetting. VERIFIED (snippet). "<1,000 years insufficient" is a senken.io paraphrase: UNVERIFIED.
- **EU CRCF:** Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 adopted late 2024 (UNVERIFIED, memory). First delegated act for permanent removals (DACCS, BioCCS, biochar) adopted about 3 Feb 2026, OJ 17 Apr 2026, in force 7 May 2026. Carbon farming delegated act C(2026)4666 (agriculture/agroforestry on mineral soils, peatland rewetting, afforestation) adopted per one source; dates unclear. EU registry by 2028. Carbon Market Watch and others (June 2026) requested internal review of the permanent-removals act; Oeko-Institut (May 2026) found the carbon farming method weak vs Article 6.4 standards. https://esgpost.com/eu-adopts-first-certification-rules-for-permanent-carbon-removals/ ; https://chemxplore.com/news/commission-adopts-certification-methodologies-carbon-farming ; https://www.hsfkramer.com/it/notes/energy/2026-posts/a-new-eu-framework-for-carbon-removals-and-carbon-farming ; https://www.robinwood.de/sites/default/files/2026-06-12-crcf-request-for-internal-review.pdf ; https://www.oeko.de/fileadmin/oekodoc/Analysis-EU-CRCF-carbon-farming-methodology.pdf.
